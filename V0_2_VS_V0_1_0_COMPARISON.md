## 2. Task-by-Task Walkthrough Comparison (`v0.1.0` vs. `v0.2`)

### Task 1: First-Time Visitor Orientation ("What is this site and what can I ask?")
- **In `v0.1.0`**: Visitor lands on a page showing a technical verification bar (`3-Axis Verification PASS · 42/42 source digests verified · Data SHA-256 4c21b49e0c46…`), a pre-loaded Strait of Hormuz briefing table (`SUPPORTED · 8/10 sources active`), and 6 citizen guide boxes before reaching the inquiry tabs.
- **In `v0.2`**: Visitor lands directly on **"What does history say?"** with the supporting text *"Something happening today sound familiar? Explore what happened before, what people believed, what they tried, and what happened next."* Below the search box are 5 everyday example questions (`🏦 What happened when people panicked about banks?`, `🚢 What if oil & gas tankers are blocked in the Persian Gulf?`, `🚰 Did taking the handle off the London water pump stop cholera?`, `🏭 What turned the 1911 Triangle Fire into workplace safety laws?`, `🕊️ Which peace or ceasefire arrangements protected civilians—and why did others fail?`) and 5 visual story cards.

### Task 2: One-Minute Comprehension ("Give me the basic story of the 1854 London cholera outbreak in ~1 minute")
- **In `v0.1.0`**: No 1-minute view existed. The visitor had to read or skim the 6-cell Citizen Guide plus multiple tabs (`Facts`, `Arguments`, `Timeline`, `Outcomes`, `Compare`) totaling over 1,600 words.
- **In `v0.2`**: Clicking **`Quick Look (~1 min read)`** renders all 6 story steps (`1. The Question`, `2. Back Then`, `3. The Debate`, `4. What Happened`, `5. What's Different Today`, `6. Explore the Evidence`) in a single 6-card grid (~215 narrative words) with clickable source chips (`[1] John Snow`, `[2] Cholera Inquiry Committee`, `[6] CDC`) and a 1-click button to jump into any step of the full interactive journey.

### Task 3: Understanding Historical Uncertainty & Hindsight ("What did people at the time believe would happen vs. what actually happened?")
- **In `v0.1.0`**: Clicking the `Arguments` tab showed a dense 4-column table where the outcome was already visible next to the expectation.
- **In `v0.2`**: Clicking **`Step 3: The Debate`** shows each historical debate as an interactive card:
  1. **What people argued at the time** (e.g., Snow arguing to the St. James Parish Board on Sept 7, 1854 that the outbreak pointed to the Broad Street pump vs. miasma theorists).
  2. **What policymakers expected would happen** (precautionary handle removal on Sept 8 while officials remained unconvinced water alone explained the outbreak).
  3. **Interactive reflection prompt**: *"Before revealing the record: What do you think happened next?"* (`Held up as expected` / `Held up with key limits` / `Refuted by events` / `Reveal documented outcome ↓`).
  4. **Documented outcome & historiographical verdict**: Reveals that daily deaths had already peaked around Sept 1–2 and declined steeply before Sept 8 (`EXPECTATION SUPPORTED (WITH LIMITS)`), alongside later historians' conclusions and analogy limits.

### Task 4: Inspecting Primary vs. Secondary Evidence & Consequential Correction
- **In `v0.1.0`**: Challenging a source required scrolling to the 8-tab workbench, opening the `Notebook` tab, and clicking the consequential correction button.
- **In `v0.2`**: In **`Step 6: Explore the Evidence`**, the visitor can try a guided **Source-Investigation Challenge** comparing two sources in the case (e.g., Snow's 1855 mortality table `snow1855` vs. the CDC's 2004 summary `cdc2004`; or the 1911 NYT fire report `tri-nyt1911` vs. the 1915 Factory Investigating Commission report `tri-fic1912`), click **`Test Consequential Correction on cdc2004 in Notebook`**, and watch the entire interface—including `Quick Look`, `Explore`, and `Examine the Evidence`—immediately narrow dependent claims to surviving primary records.

# Research-to-Design Decision Record — OpenLine Department of History v0.2

**Baseline Compared**: OpenLine Department of History `v0.1.0` (commit `b1686b284b17f814781342547547dda5af90f0bb`, tag `v0.1.0` / `8e420a2045368b224147cb321b9b148e72ece56f`, live at [terryncew.github.io/openline-department-of-history](https://terryncew.github.io/openline-department-of-history/), source at [github.com/terryncew/openline-department-of-history](https://github.com/terryncew/openline-department-of-history)) and the Competent Conventional Searchable Historical Reference Interface (`dist/conventional_baseline_brief.html`).

## 1. Executive Summary: What Research Changed the Product

In `v0.1.0`, the OpenLine evidence engine achieved strong scholarly rigor (5 curated inquiries, 42 verified sources, 104 core records, 15 comparable argument chains, 3-axis verification, analogy falsification, and consequential source-withdrawal propagation), but its **front door and primary reading flow were built for specialist auditors rather than ordinary citizens, teenagers, families, and curious adults**:

- Opening `v0.1.0` immediately rendered **4,114 visible words** and **9,962 px of vertical scroll** (11.7 screens on desktop), leading with SHA-256 byte-integrity badges (`3-Axis Verification PASS · 42/42 source digests verified · Data SHA-256 4c21b49e0c46…`), a pre-expanded multi-table Strait of Hormuz briefing, a 6-card analytical dossier summary, and an 8-tab specialist toolbar (`Facts`, `Arguments`, `Timeline`, `Compare`, `Outcomes`, `Historians`, `Falsify`, `Notebook`).
- First-time visitors had to parse academic terminology (`Confounders`, `Counterfactual gap`, `Historiographical divergence`, `3-Axis Verification`) before encountering the human story or understanding what people at the time believed and argued.

Before writing code for `v0.2`, we reviewed empirical research and public-sector standards across six domains:
1. **Consumer-grade mobile interfaces and progressive disclosure**: Nielsen Norman Group ([Progressive Disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/), [Defer Secondary Content When Writing for Mobile Users](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/defer-secondary-content-for-mobile/), and [4 Principles to Reduce Cognitive Load](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/4-principles-reduce-cognitive-load/)).
2. **Plain-language civic communication and step-by-step orientation**: Digital.gov ([Principles of Plain Language](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/principles), [An Introduction to Plain Language](https://digital.gov/resources/an-introduction-to-plain-language/)) and the U.S. Web Design System ([USWDS Step Indicator](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/step-indicator/), [USWDS Process List](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/process-list/), and [USWDS Accordion](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/accordion/)).
3. **Primary-source historical inquiry**: Smithsonian Institution ([Activating the Learning Potential of Digitized Museum Objects with the Smithsonian Learning Lab](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/blogs/smithsonian-institution-office-fellowships-and-int/2023/04/10/activating-the-learning-potential-of-digitized-museum-objects-with-the-smithsonian-learning-lab/) and [Teachers Transform Learning With the Smithsonian at Their Fingertips](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/blogs/smithsonian-education/2023/02/14/smithsonian-learning-lab/)) and the Stanford History Education Group / Digital Inquiry Group ([Historical Thinking Chart](https://www.inquirygroup.org/history-lessons/historical-thinking-chart) and Sam Wineburg, [Teaching Historical Thinking Using Primary Sources](https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/teachers/about-this-program/teaching-with-primary-sources-partner-program/documents/historical_thinking.pdf)).
4. **Interactive museum exhibit label hierarchy**: Beverly Serrell's museum exhibit label architecture and three-tier interactive museum touchscreen hierarchy ([Interactive Touchscreens in Museums & Galleries Guide](https://digitalyearbook.org/blog/interactive-touchscreens-museums-galleries-guide/)).
5. **Accessible timelines, visual diagrams, and motion discipline**: W3C Web Accessibility Initiative ([WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices](https://wai-aria-practices.netlify.app/aria-practices/)) and WCAG 2.2 keyboard/pointer guidelines ([Build Accessible Carousels That Pass WCAG 2.2 Audits](https://wcagpros.com/wcag-guidelines/build-accessible-carousels-that-pass-wcag-22-audits-effortlessly/)).
6. **Educational engagement without manipulative gamification**: Self-Determination Theory research on intrinsic motivation vs. extrinsic point systems ([Impacts of Gamification on Intrinsic Motivation](https://www.ntnu.edu/documents/139799/1279149990/04+Article+Final_camildah_fors%C3%B8k_2017-12-06-13-53-55_TPD4505.Camilla.Dahlstr%C3%B8m.pdf)).

## 3. What We Deliberately Refused to Introduce

1. **No Frontend Framework or Build-Chain Bloat**: We retained zero-dependency vanilla JavaScript (`engine.js`, `app.js`), semantic HTML5 (`template.html`), and CSS (`styles.css`), compiled deterministically via Python standard library (`scripts/build.py`) into a single offline-capable `dist/index.html` (`522,306` bytes) that runs directly from `file://` with **zero external network requests**.
2. **No Generative AI / LLM Runtime Dependency**: Ordinary-language question routing (`Engine.askHistory`), progressive depth generation (`Engine.buildStoryJourney`), analogy falsification (`Engine.evaluateComparisonCandidate`), and consequential source-withdrawal propagation (`Engine.evaluateConsequentialCorrection`) are 100% deterministic, auditable, and source-bound. Unsupported or future-forecast questions fail closed (`UNSUPPORTED`) rather than hallucinating an answer.
3. **No Fabricated Quotes, Motives, or Photographs**: Every interactive timeline step, debate reveal, plain-language concept card, archival reconstruction diagram, and source-investigation challenge is bound to verified `sourceIds` in `dist/data.json`. All 5 SVG diagrams carry an explicit `DOCUMENTED_ARCHIVAL_RECONSTRUCTION_DIAGRAM — NOT_A_PHOTOGRAPH` provenance banner citing the underlying primary records, and the original public-domain [Emergency Banking Act of March 9, 1933 PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-48/pdf/STATUTE-48-Pg1.pdf) (`48 Stat. 1`) remains bundled offline in `dist/originals/emergency-banking-act-1933.pdf`.
